Rim Locks on Dirt Bikes: What They Do and When You Need One

November 17, 2026 · By Zenyare Tire
𝕏 Share Facebook Copy Link

Who we are: Tire of Moto is run by ZENYARE (Qingdao Zhengya Rubber Industrial Co., Ltd.), a manufacturer of motorcycle tires, inner tubes and mousse inserts. We're a manufacturer, not a neutral test lab, and we haven't tested any rim locks for this article. What follows comes from the published instructions of rim lock, mousse and motorcycle makers, with sources at the end. There are no shopping links.

The short answer: a rim lock clamps the tire's bead against the inside of the rim so the tire can't turn on the wheel. If you run a mousse, the mousse maker we checked says you must have one. With a tube, a tire that slips on the rim shows up as a tilted valve stem, and Honda's 2022 CRF250R/RX owner's manual lists "Rim locks for looseness" in its checks before each ride. Choose the rim lock by your rim's width, fit it the way the maker says, and take tire pressures from your owner's manual.

What does a rim lock do?

A rim lock sits inside the tire, between the rim and the tire bead. A bolt passes through a hole in the rim, and a nut on the outside pulls the rim lock against the bead. That clamps a short section of the bead to the rim.

Its job is to stop the tire moving around the rim. Nuetech, which makes Nitromousse inserts, says a proper rim lock must be used "to prevent tire slip." On its website it calls the rim lock "an essential safety feature of a dirt bike wheel."

Mousse gel on the bead is one thing Michelin warns about. Michelin's mousse instructions warn against getting gel on the tire beads because of the "risk of rotation on the rim."

Why does it matter if the tire turns on the rim?

What happens depends on what's inside the tire.

Inside the tireWhat the makers say
Inner tubeHonda: "A tilted valve stem indicates the tube is slipping inside the tyre or the tyre is slipping on the rim. See your dealer."
MousseNuetech: a proper fitting rim lock must be used "to prevent tire slip"; do not mount a Nitromousse without one
TUblissNuetech says TUbliss "acts like a full circumference rim lock," and its listed weights include a rim lock

The tube row is the one new riders meet most. The valve stem goes through the rim and is part of the tube. If the tire and tube creep around the rim, the stem gets pulled sideways. Honda's instruction is simple: if the valve stem is tilted, something is slipping, and the bike should go to a dealer.

Do I need a rim lock?

Start with what your bike came with, then look at what you'll put in the tire.

  1. Check your owner's manual. Look for rim locks in your manual's checks. Honda's 2022 CRF250R/RX manual, for example, lists "Rim locks for looseness" in the checks before each ride. If your manual lists rim locks, keep them fitted and keep checking them.
  2. If you're switching to mousse, follow the mousse maker. Nuetech says never to mount a Nitromousse without a proper fitting and functioning rim lock. Check your own mousse maker's instructions.
  3. If you're fitting TUbliss, Nuetech's product page says TUbliss "acts like a full circumference rim lock" and lists its weights "INCLUDING rim lock." Follow the TUbliss instructions.
  4. If your bike has no rim lock and you run tubes, ask your motorcycle maker or dealer before adding one. Adding one changes the wheel, so follow the wheel maker's instructions or have a shop do it.

Whatever you run, look at the valve stem when you check the bike. A tilted stem is Honda's sign that something is slipping.

Front, rear or both?

Follow the bike maker's setup. Warp 9 lists front widths of 1.60" and 1.85" and rear widths of 2.15", 2.50" and 3.00". The rim lock has to match the rim it's fitted to, so if your front and rear rims are different widths, they need different rim locks.

How do I choose the right rim lock size?

By rim width. Warp 9, for example, sizes its rim locks by the width of the rim they fit, not by tire size. Warp 9 sells its rim locks in 1.40", 1.60", 1.85", 2.15", 2.50" and 3.00" rim widths. It also says the base washer "is machined to match each specific rim curvature."

Find your rim width in your bike's spec sheet or owner's manual, or ask your dealer. Don't guess it from the tire size.

How do you install a rim lock?

Follow the maker's instructions for your rim lock and for whatever goes inside the tire. Nuetech's mousse instructions give the order that matters most:

  1. Bead under the rim lock first. Start fitting the tire by "securing the tire bead under the rim lock."
  2. Work the bead on around the rim. The rest of the bead goes into the drop centre of the rim with tire irons.
  3. Seat the tire, then tighten. Stand the wheel up and push down on the rim lock to make sure the tire is fully seated. Only "with the tire mounted properly," tighten down the rim lock.
  4. Plug unused holes. Nuetech says to plug any unused holes in the rim so lubricant can't seep out.

Before every ride, check the rim lock nut for looseness, as Honda's pre-ride list for the CRF250R/RX does. We don't give a tightening torque here, because it depends on the rim lock and the bike; ask your dealer.

Do rim locks change tire pressure?

A rim lock doesn't set your pressure. Take the pressure from your owner's manual, and make any changes for terrain the way your manual says. Honda's manual says to make changes "a little at a time." With mousse, there's no air pressure to set at all.

Rim lock checklist

  1. Read your owner's manual: does your bike have rim locks?
  2. Decide what goes in the tire (tube, mousse or TUbliss) and read that maker's instructions.
  3. Find the width of each rim in your spec sheet or owner's manual.
  4. Buy a rim lock sized for that rim width.
  5. Fit the bead under the rim lock first; tighten the nut only after the tire is seated.
  6. Check the nut and the valve stem angle before every ride.

FAQ

What does a rim lock do on a dirt bike?

It clamps the tire bead to the rim so the tire can't turn on the wheel. Nuetech says a rim lock is used "to prevent tire slip."

Do I need a rim lock with a mousse?

The mousse maker we checked says yes. Nuetech says not to mount a Nitromousse without a proper fitting and functioning rim lock. Check your mousse maker's instructions.

How do I know if my tire is slipping on the rim?

Look at the valve stem. Honda says a tilted valve stem means the tube is slipping inside the tire or the tire is slipping on the rim, and to see your dealer.

When do you tighten the rim lock?

After the tire is fitted and seated. Nuetech's instructions say to push down on the rim lock to seat the tire, then tighten it.

What size rim lock do I need?

One that matches your rim's width. Warp 9, for example, sells sizes such as 1.60" and 2.15"; find your rim width in your bike's spec sheet or owner's manual.
