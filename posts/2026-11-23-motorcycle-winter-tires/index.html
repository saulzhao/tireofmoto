Do Motorcycles Need Winter Tires? M+S, the Snowflake Mark and What the Rules Say

November 23, 2026 · By Zenyare Tire
𝕏 Share Facebook Copy Link

Who we are: Tire of Moto is run by ZENYARE (Qingdao Zhengya Rubber Industrial Co., Ltd.), a manufacturer of motorcycle tires, inner tubes and mousse inserts. Nothing below promotes our products. What follows comes from tire makers' and regulators' own texts, with sources at the end.

Some tire makers list winter tires for two-wheelers. The ones we found on a maker's own site are scooter sizes with M+S (mud and snow) in their names. Not every maker does: Michelin says it designs and manufactures winter tyres for four-wheeled vehicles, and that on a motorcycle "it's not just a question of grip."

As for the law, the one official text we checked in full is Germany's. Its winter tire rule for icy and snowy roads does not apply to single-track motor vehicles, but the same paragraph sets extra conditions for driving a vehicle without such tires in those conditions. We didn't check other countries' rules, so we don't cover them here.

Yes. The clearest example we found on a maker's own site is Heidenau, which lists two families under "Winter Scooter Tires" on its online shop:

Michelin takes a different position. On its UK site it says: "At Michelin, we design and manufacture winter tyres for four-wheeled vehicles," and explains that "on a motorcycle, it's not just a question of grip. The other parameter that comes into play is balance." Our guide to motorcycle tire grip in cold weather covers Michelin's advice on riding in snow.

What does M+S mean on a motorcycle tire?

M+S marks a snow tire. The original 1988 text of UN Regulation No. 75, the UN rule for motorcycle and moped tires, defines a "snow tyre" as one "whose tread pattern and whose structure are primarily designed to ensure in mud and fresh or melting snow a performance better than that of an ordinary (road-type) tyre."

The same text requires "the inscription of "M + S" or "M.S" or "M & S" in the case of a snow tyre." So you may see any of those three spellings on a sidewall.

Mitas defines it in plainer words: "M+S – tyres designed for use in mud and snow (winter tyres)."

The definition is about design intent compared with an ordinary road tire. It isn't a performance score, and the marking alone doesn't tell you how a tire behaves on ice.

What is the snowflake symbol, and do motorcycle tires have it?

The snowflake symbol is a mountain with a snowflake inside it. German law calls it the "Alpine-Symbol (Bergpiktogramm mit Schneeflocke)", meaning the Alpine symbol, a mountain pictogram with a snowflake.

Germany's vehicle regulations (StVZO §36(4)) define tires for wintry conditions as tires that improve driving on snow and that are "marked with the Alpine symbol … under UNECE Regulation No. 117" (our translation). UN Regulation No. 117 is a different regulation from the motorcycle tire rule, No. 75.

The same section, in paragraph 11, refers separately to "M+S Reifen" (M+S tyres) that meet the description in point 2.29 of Regulation No. 75, which the StVZO cites by its title for tyres for motorcycles and mopeds ("Luftreifen für Krafträder und Mopeds"), when they're used on class L vehicles. That paragraph deals with how the speed rating rule applies to them.

The Heidenau winter tire pages we read describe the tires by their M+S names and don't mention a snowflake symbol. We didn't find a maker page showing a snowflake mark on a motorcycle tire. If a snowflake matters to you, check the sidewall of the actual tire or ask the maker.

Does Germany require winter tires on motorcycles?

Germany's road traffic regulations (StVO §2(3a)) say a motor vehicle may only be driven in "Glatteis, Schneeglätte, Schneematsch, Eisglätte oder Reifglätte" (black ice, slippery snow, slush, slippery ice or hoarfrost) if all wheels have tires that meet StVZO §36(4). The next sentence says this does not apply to several vehicle types, including "einspurige Kraftfahrzeuge": single-track motor vehicles.

The paragraph doesn't stop there. It goes on to say that where a vehicle may be driven in those conditions without such tires, the driver must, in addition to their general duties (our translation):

  1. before each trip, check whether the trip is necessary because the destination can't be reached by other means of transport;
  2. while driving, keep a distance in metres to the vehicle ahead of at least half the speed shown on the speedometer in km/h;
  3. not drive faster than 50 km/h, unless a lower speed is required.

How German authorities apply this to a particular rider is for them to decide; we're quoting the text, not giving legal advice. If you ride in Germany in winter, read §2 of the StVO yourself (link below).

What about other countries?

We only read Germany's text for this article. Winter tire rules differ between countries and regions. Check the official traffic regulations wherever you ride, rather than relying on a summary.

Before you fit winter tires on a scooter or motorcycle

  • Check that the maker lists your exact size. The Heidenau ranges above cover small-wheel sizes only.
  • Match the load and speed markings your owner's manual specifies. Our guide to motorcycle tire size markings explains how to read them.
  • Set pressures from your owner's manual or the label on the bike.

FAQ

Are there winter tires for motorcycles?

Some makers list M+S winter tires for two-wheelers. Heidenau, for example, lists its K62 and K66 Snowtex ranges under "Winter Scooter Tires". Michelin says its winter tires are for four-wheeled vehicles.

Does Germany require winter tires on motorcycles?

StVO §2(3a) says the winter tire requirement for icy and snowy roads does not apply to single-track motor vehicles. The same paragraph sets extra conditions when a vehicle is driven in those conditions without winter tires, including a 50 km/h maximum.

What is the difference between M+S and the snowflake symbol?

M+S is the snow tire marking defined in UN Regulation No. 75, the motorcycle tire regulation. The snowflake (Alpine) symbol is defined under UN Regulation No. 117, which Germany uses to define tires for wintry conditions.

Is it safe to ride a motorcycle on snow with regular tires?

We can only tell you what makers and regulators say. Michelin says that on a motorcycle in snow "it's not just a question of grip" but also balance, and adds: "We recommend that you avoid riding a motorbike in snowy conditions." Our cold-weather guide covers Michelin's advice in more detail. German law sets extra conditions for driving without winter tires on snowy and icy roads.
