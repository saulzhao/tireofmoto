Storing a Motorcycle for Winter: What Tire Makers Say About Flat Spots, Sunlight and Ozone

November 25, 2026 · By Zenyare Tire
𝕏 Share Facebook Copy Link

Who we are: Tire of Moto is run by ZENYARE (Qingdao Zhengya Rubber Industrial Co., Ltd.), a manufacturer of motorcycle tires, inner tubes and mousse inserts. Nothing below promotes our products. What follows comes from tire makers' and motorcycle makers' own texts, with sources at the end.

Parking a bike for three or four months is easy. Getting it back in spring with tires that still look and feel right takes a little planning. Tire and motorcycle makers publish storage advice. Much of it overlaps, but not every detail matches. Below, each point says who said it.

The short answer

  • Get the weight off the tires if you can. Michelin, Harley-Davidson and Continental all suggest a stand.
  • No stand? Move the bike now and then so the same patch of rubber isn't loaded all winter. How often depends on whose advice you follow.
  • Keep tires away from sunlight, ozone sources, solvents and damp. Dunlop and Continental both list electric motors among the ozone sources.
  • Tire pressure: makers don't give the same advice. See the pressure section below, and use your owner's manual.
  • Check everything before the first ride.

Why tires need attention over winter

Michelin's FAQ lists "poor storage conditions" among the physical threats to a tire, and "extreme temperatures, humidity, ozone, solvents and chemicals" among the environmental ones. Its winter storage guide adds the problem specific to a parked bike: "extended periods of weight on one contact point can lead to flat spots and rubber damage."

Dunlop UK is blunter: "Tyres can be damaged as a result of poor storage conditions. This damage can affect tyre performance and function and may eventually lead to tyre failure."

Flat spots: use a stand, or move the bike

All three makers below say the same basic thing, getting the weight off the tires, but they word the fallback differently:

WhoWith a standWithout a stand
Michelin (USA)"The most effective tire protection comes from fully elevating the tires off the ground using a stand.""move the motorcycle forward or backward slightly from time to time to change the tire contact point"
Harley-Davidson"store your motorcycle on a center stand during the winter""move your motorcycle and rotate your tires at least once per month"
Continental (Australia)"store the vehicle on the main stand or on a motorcycle jack to remove all weight from the tyres""completely unload the vehicle," and move it "every month to minimize ozone cracking in the contact area and also to minimize a 'flat spot' from developing"

Continental also mentions something the others don't: covering the tires "to protect them from environmental exposure" while the bike is on its stand.

If a flat spot does form, two makers say it usually doesn't last. Continental: "If the tyres do develop 'flat spots,' they will usually disappear in a short period of service." Michelin Canada says flat spots "typically self-correct after 10–15 minutes of gentle riding as rubber warms." That's their wording, not a guarantee, so still inspect the tires (see the spring section).

Which stand suits your bike, and how to lift it safely, is in your owner's manual. We don't recommend a particular stand here.

Tire pressure for winter storage

This is where the makers differ, so here is exactly what each one says.

Michelin (USA), bike stored on its tires: "Cold temperatures cause air to contract, leading to lower tire pressure. Tires with insufficient pressure are more susceptible to flat spots during long storage periods. Before storage, inflate tires to the manufacturer's recommended PSI, listed in the owner's manual or on the motorcycle."

Continental (Australia), bike stored on its tires: "it is permissible to inflate the tyres to the maximum pressure listed on the sidewall. Be sure to return the inflation pressure to recommended usage pressure before operating the vehicle."

Continental (Australia), wheels with tires stored off the bike: "Mounted tyres should preferably be inflated to only 15 psi. Be sure to adjust the tyres to the recommended inflation pressure before mounting on the vehicle."

So one maker says recommended pressure, the other says sidewall maximum is "permissible" for a bike resting on its tires. Both agree on one point: before you ride, the tires go back to the pressure in your owner's manual or on the bike's label. If you're unsure which storage advice applies to your tires, follow your owner's manual and ask your tire's maker. Harley-Davidson's guide, for its part, just says to "ensure that tires are properly inflated."

Where to keep the bike

Dunlop UK says stored tyres should be kept "where the area is clean, dry, well ventilated and the ambient temperatures are temperate," and not where they are "exposed to direct sunlight, extreme hot/cold temperatures or ozone-generating sources such as electric motors, battery chargers, generators or welding equipment."

That last item matters in a garage. Michelin's winter guide suggests a battery tender or trickle charger for the parked bike, and Dunlop names battery chargers as an ozone source to keep tires away from, so it's worth thinking about where the charger sits. Continental likewise says to avoid storing tyres "in the same area as an electric motor or other ozone generating sources."

Michelin's guide adds that "temperature extremes, moisture, and direct sunlight can accelerate rubber deterioration."

The rest of winterizing (fuel, battery, oil) varies by model. Michelin's own guide says to "always consult your owner's manual for manufacturer-specific winter storage recommendations."

Storing loose tires or spare wheels

If you're storing a spare set, or wheels you've taken off, the same environmental advice applies, plus a few points from Continental's motorcycle and scooter tyre page:

  • "DO store tyres where it is clean, dark, dry and moderately ventilated."
  • "DO store all motorcycle tyres at temperatures not exceeding 35°C, preferably below 25°C."
  • Avoid storing conventional motorcycle tyres "at temperatures well below the freezing point (max. -20°C)." Continental sets a stricter limit for its race tyres.
  • "ALWAYS allow tyres - which have been exposed to 0°C or less - to warm to room temperature (approx. 20°C) for a minimum of 24 hours before the tyres are mounted or flexed."
  • For tires mounted on wheels, the 15 psi guidance quoted above.

About "dry rot": riders often call the fine cracks on old or badly stored tires dry rot. Dunlop UK calls it "ozone cracking or weather damage," and Continental also mentions "ozone cracking." Two things help, according to Dunlop UK and Continental. Keep tires away from sunlight and ozone sources, as above. And don't coat them: Dunlop UK says to clean sidewalls with "a mild soap solution" and "never apply any other material, cleaners or dressings to enhance sidewall appearance," because these "may degrade the rubber and make it impossible to check for ozone cracking or weather damage."

Storing new tires in a warehouse before export is a different job. Our sister site covers it in tire storage before shipment.

Before the first ride in spring

Michelin Canada answers the question directly. Can you reuse tires after winter storage? "Yes, if they pass inspection." Its checklist: flat spots, sidewall cracking, tread depth ("if at or near the wear limit before storage, replace now"), and pressure, which "will have dropped during cold storage—inflate to spec."

Michelin's US spring guide adds to check "for cracking, uneven wear, or flat spots, particularly if the motorcycle was stored without a stand." Harley-Davidson makes the related point before storage: "Overly worn tires might not make it through the winter."

FAQ

How should I store motorcycle tires over winter?

Dry, clean, ventilated, out of direct sunlight and away from ozone sources such as electric motors, battery chargers and generators (Dunlop UK). Continental adds a maximum storage temperature of 35°C for motorcycle tyres, preferably below 25°C.

Should I put my motorcycle on a stand for winter storage?

Michelin, Harley-Davidson and Continental all suggest a stand to take weight off the tires. Without one, they say to move the bike periodically: Harley-Davidson says at least once per month, Continental says every month.

What tire pressure should I use for winter storage?

Makers differ. Michelin says inflate to the manufacturer's recommended pressure. Continental says that for a bike resting on its tires, sidewall maximum is "permissible," and for wheels stored off the bike, "preferably" 15 psi. Both say to set the recommended pressure before riding.

Can tires get flat spots when a motorcycle sits all winter?

Yes. Michelin says flat spots "may develop" when a bike rests on its tires for long periods, and that low pressure makes them more likely. Continental and Michelin Canada say flat spots usually go away after a short period of riding. Inspect the tires before you ride.
