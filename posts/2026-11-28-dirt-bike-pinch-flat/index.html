Pinch Flats on Dirt Bike Tubes: Why Snake Bites Happen and How to Prevent Them

November 28, 2026 · By Zenyare Tire
𝕏 Share Facebook Copy Link

Who we are: Tire of Moto is run by ZENYARE (Qingdao Zhengya Rubber Industrial Co., Ltd.), a manufacturer of motorcycle tires, inner tubes and mousse inserts. Nothing below promotes our products. What follows comes from tire makers' own texts, with sources at the end.

You fix a flat on the trail, and the hole in the tube isn't a thorn hole. It's a short cut, often with a second one right beside it. Riders call this a "snake bite." It's a pinch flat, and it has a cause you can usually do something about.

The short answer

Michelin describes a pinch flat as "the tube gets pinched between the tire and rim when the tire compresses against a hard obstacle." Michelin lists pinch flats as one of the two main risks of running too low a pressure off-road. Michelin's advice for tubes is to stay "within the range specified for the tire and terrain — lower than road pressure but within a defined range, not as low as possible."

A tube can also get pinched while you mount the tire. Dunlop's mounting instructions include steps aimed at exactly that.

What happens in a pinch flat

When the tire hits a rock edge, a square-edged root or the lip of a rut, it squashes. If there's enough air in it, the tire takes the hit. If there isn't, the tire squashes far enough that the tube is caught between the tire and the rim, and the rubber is cut.

Michelin puts it this way in its off-road pressure FAQ: going too low "risks pinch punctures when the tire is compressed against rocks or ruts, and rim damage." It lists the two risks of too-low pressure as pinch flats and rim damage, where "the rim strikes rocks or hard edges that the under-pressured tire can't cushion."

So if you find a snake bite, the rim may have taken a hard hit too. Michelin's advice after a muddy session includes: "Inspect the rim for any impact damage."

Cause 1: pressure too low for the terrain

Dropping pressure off-road is normal. Michelin says lowering it slightly "increases the tire's contact area with the ground, which can improve grip on loose or soft terrain," and calls this "common practice in enduro and motocross."

The catch is how far. Michelin's guidance:

  • "Always consult the specific off-road tire's documentation for the recommended pressure range for different terrain types, and never reduce below a safe minimum."
  • "Harder, rockier surfaces typically require slightly higher pressure to protect against rim and tire damage."
  • "Check pressure before every ride, with the tires cold (not ridden in the last few hours)."

We don't give a pressure number here. Take it from your tire maker's off-road pressure information for your tire and terrain, and from your owner's manual. If you ride to the trail on the road, Michelin says to "reinflate to the manufacturer's recommended road pressure before riding on tarmac."

Cause 2: the tube was pinched during mounting

Not every pinch happens on the trail. A tube can be caught between the tire bead and the rim, or under a tire iron, while you fit the tire. Dunlop's Tire Care page has several precautions about this:

  • Give the tube a little shape. Dunlop: "Leave just enough air in the tube for installing, so the tube holds its shape."
  • Keep it even. Dunlop: "Be sure the tube is not bunched up in any area or caught under the rim locks." Its general mounting precautions also say: "Centralize rim band and tube to prevent pinching if tube-type rim."
  • Watch the irons. Dunlop: "Stick the iron in just far enough past the rim edge being careful not to catch the tube."
  • Let it settle. Dunlop: "After the tire is fully inflated, let the air out again then re-inflate. This allows the tube to settle inside the tire." Its precautions also say that for a tube-type tire you should "deflate and reinflate after seating to prevent tube wrinkles."

We're not giving the full mounting procedure here. Dunlop's step-by-step off-road tire removal and installation is on the same Tire Care page. If the bead won't seat while you inflate, see why a motorcycle tire bead won't seat.

Cause 3: the wrong tube, or an old one

Two more points from Dunlop's Tubes section:

  • "Always check the size markings on the tube to ensure that the tire size appears on the tube."
  • "When fitting a new tire on a rim requiring a tube, a new tube should be fitted at the same time. Old tubes become stretched and if an old tube is fitted within a new tire, it can crease and fail due to thinning of the tube rubber."

Dunlop doesn't call that a pinch flat, but a creased, thinned tube is one more reason a tube can fail after a tire change.

Other ways to take the tube out of the equation

  • Mousse inserts replace the tube and the air. Michelin says "a punctured tire with a mousse continues to function," and also that mousses are "not a universal substitute for inner tubes and require the correct combination of tire and mousse size." Our mousse vs tube guide covers them.
  • Rim locks do a different job: they stop the tire turning on the rim. See rim locks on dirt bikes.

FAQ

What causes a pinch flat on a dirt bike?

Michelin explains it as the tube being "pinched between the tire and rim when the tire compresses against a hard obstacle." Michelin names pinch flats as one of the two main risks of running too low a pressure off-road. A tube can also be pinched while the tire is being mounted.

How do I avoid pinching the tube when mounting a dirt bike tire?

Dunlop's precautions: leave just enough air in the tube so it holds its shape, make sure it isn't bunched up or caught under the rim locks, keep tire irons "just far enough past the rim edge," and deflate and re-inflate after the beads seat so the tube settles. The full procedure is on Dunlop's Tire Care page.

Does a heavy-duty tube stop pinch flats?

The makers we checked don't say so. Michelin says its 2.5 mm reinforced and 4 mm UHD tubes make the tire more robust, and Motoz says the rubber in its tubes can be compressed under load without splitting. None of them promises a tube that can't pinch. Keep pressure within the range for your tire and terrain.

What pressure should I run to avoid pinch flats?

We don't give a number. Michelin's advice is to use the range "specified for the tire and terrain," to run slightly higher on hard, rocky ground, and never go "below a safe minimum." Check your tire maker's off-road pressure information and your owner's manual.

Can I patch a pinch flat?

Dunlop says "Tubes should be repaired only by an expert," and that a new tube should go in with every new tire.
