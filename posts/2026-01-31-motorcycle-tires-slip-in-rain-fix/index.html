Motorcycle Tires Slip in Rain: How to Fix Wet Grip Issues

January 30, 2026·Read article
❉ ShareFacebookCopy Link

Introduction

If you're looking for tire information for the Motorcycle Tires Slip in Rain: How to Fix Wet Grip Issues, you've come to the right place. Whether doing a scheduled replacement or upgrading your ride, knowing exactly which tire sizes and types fit your motorcycle is critical for safety.

Correct Tire Specifications

The manufacturer specifies exact front and rear tire sizes for each model year. Standard specifications include optimized contact patch width, load indexes meeting OEM weight requirements, and speed ratings sufficient for legal road speeds with a safety margin.

Why Correct Tire Size Matters

Using the wrong tire size can cause incorrect speedometer reading, altered steering geometry, reduced ground clearance, brake interference, and potential suspension damage. Always use manufacturer-specified sizes or consult a tire professional for approved alternatives.

Best Tire Options for This Model

When shopping for replacement tires, consider your riding style:

  1. Daily commuting: Wet weather grip ratings and long tread life compounds
  2. Sport riding: Softer compounds with aggressive tread patterns for cornering grip
  3. Touring: High-mileage ratings and hydroplaning resistance
  4. Dual-sport: Mixed street/off-road tread handling gravel and trails

Replacement Tips

Always check the DOT manufacture date (last 4 digits on sidewall). Tires older than 5 years should be replaced regardless of tread depth. Have your bike's tire mounted and balanced by a professional.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use a different tire size than stock?

Minor deviations are sometimes possible but can affect ABS calibration and speedometer accuracy. Consult the tire manufacturer's fitment guide first.

How often should motorcycle tires be replaced?

Every 3-5 years by age or when tread reaches wear bars, whichever comes first. Typical mileage: 3,000 miles (sport) to 20,000+ miles (touring).

What tire pressure should I set?

Check your owner manual or swingarm sticker. Front tires generally run lower PSI than rear tires.
