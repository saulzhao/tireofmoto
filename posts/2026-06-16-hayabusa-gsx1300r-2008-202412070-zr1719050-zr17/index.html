Suzuki Hayabusa GSX1300R Tire Sizes: What Suzuki Publishes for Gen 2 and Gen 3

Published on June 16, 2026

Every Suzuki specification we could find for the Hayabusa from 2008 onwards lists the same tire sizes: 120/70ZR17M/C (58W) at the front and 190/50ZR17M/C (73W) at the rear. That covers the second generation launched for 2008, the 2014 Japanese-market model, the third generation launched in 2021, and the 2022 to 2024 product pages. Suzuki doesn't publish a separate spec page we could read for the other years, so check the owner's manual or the tires on your bike.

We only list a figure when it appears in an official Suzuki document. Anything we couldn't source is left out.

How to read these numbers

  • 120/70ZR17M/C (58W): 120 mm nominal section width, sidewall height 70% of the width, 17-inch rim, M/C for motorcycle. 58 is the load index and W the speed symbol.
  • 190/50ZR17M/C (73W): 190 mm nominal width, sidewall 50% of the width, 17-inch rim, load index 73, speed symbol W.

Suzuki lists load index 58 front and 73 rear, with speed symbol W. Check your owner's manual before choosing a replacement.

Gen 2 and Gen 3: what changed?

The tire sizes stayed the same. These are the items in Suzuki's archive tables that changed between the 2008 second generation and the 2021 third generation:

Notes from Suzuki's text:

  • 2008: "The first model change" of the Hayabusa. Suzuki says the stroke was extended by 2 mm, "boosting its cubic capacity from 1,298 to 1,340 cm³," and lists new technologies including the Suzuki Dual Throttle Valve (SDTV) system. (The spec table in the same archive gives 1339 cm³.)
  • 2014: a Japanese-specification version, "equivalent to the European spec model" in power and torque.
  • 2021: "the third-generation model," the first full model change "in 13 years," with a ride-by-wire electronic throttle and the Suzuki Intelligent Ride System (S.I.R.S.).

Dry and wet weights aren't comparable with each other.

Which years and markets does this cover?

  • Documented: 2008, 2014 (Japanese model) and 2021 in Suzuki's archive; 2022–2024 on Global Suzuki product pages.
  • Not separately documented: 2009–2013 and 2015–2020 of the second generation. The tire sizes are the same in the 2008 and 2014 tables, but we didn't find a spec page for each of those years.
  • Markets: the 2014 entry is a Japanese-market model. Specifications can differ by market, so use your owner's manual or the tire label.

Rim width and tire pressure

Suzuki's archive and product pages don't list rim widths or tire pressures, and we didn't find an official Hayabusa owner's manual we could open online. Use the pressures in your owner's manual or on the label on the bike. Don't use a figure from a forum, a different model year, or another model.

Can I fit a different size?

Only use a size approved by the motorcycle manufacturer or tire maker. Dunlop says a change should only be made "with the approval of the motorcycle or tire manufacturer."

That includes questions such as fitting a wider rear. Every Suzuki table we found lists 190/50ZR17.

Buying checklist for a Hayabusa

  1. Confirm your model year, generation and market.
  2. Compare the sizes in this table with your owner's manual or the tires fitted now.
  3. Note the load index (58 front, 73 rear) and speed symbol (W) Suzuki lists, and check them against your owner's manual.
  4. Take tire pressures from your owner's manual or the label on the bike.
  5. Follow your owner's manual on when to replace a tire.

FAQ

What tire size does a Suzuki Hayabusa take?

Suzuki's specs list 120/70ZR17M/C (58W) front and 190/50ZR17M/C (73W) rear for the 2008, 2014 and 2021 models and for 2022 to 2024.

Did the third-generation Hayabusa change tire size?

No. Suzuki's 2021 table lists the same sizes as the 2008 second generation.

Are Hayabusa tires tubeless?

The 2022–2024 Global Suzuki product pages list both tires as tubeless.

What tire pressure should I run on a Hayabusa?

Use the figures in your owner's manual or on the label on the bike. Suzuki's spec pages don't list pressures.
