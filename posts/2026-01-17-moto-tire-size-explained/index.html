How Motorcycle Tire Sizes Work (90/90-18, 3.00-18, 110/80-17)

January 17, 2026 · Read article
𝕏 Share Facebook Copy Link

Motorcycle tire sizes look confusing at first. One tire says 90/90-18, another says 3.00-18, and a third says 110/80-17. What do all these numbers mean?

This guide explains both sizing systems, shows you how to read them, and gives you practical rules to avoid buying the wrong tire.

Two common formats

Motorcycle tires use two sizing systems:

  • Metric: 90/90-18 = width 90mm / aspect 90% / rim 18”
  • Inch: 3.00-18 = approximate width 3.00” / rim 18”

Both refer to the same physical tire dimensions, just expressed differently. Understanding both is essential when shopping online or comparing brands.

Metric Format Explained

The metric format has three parts: width / aspect ratio - rim diameter.

NumberMeaningExample (90/90-18)
90 (first)Tire width in millimeters90 mm wide
90 (second)Aspect ratio (sidewall height ÷ width)Sidewall = 81mm
18Rim diameter in inchesFits 18” rim

Pro tip: To calculate sidewall height, multiply width × aspect ratio ÷ 100. For 90/90-18: 90 × 0.90 = 81mm sidewall.

Inch Format Explained

The inch format is simpler but less precise:

NumberMeaningExample (3.00-18)
3.00Tire width in inches≈ 76mm wide
18Rim diameter in inchesFits 18” rim

Note: Inch-sized tires don’t specify aspect ratio. The sidewall height is typically close to the width, making them “high profile” tires common on dual-sport and commuter bikes.

Quick Rules That Prevent Mistakes

  1. Keep rim diameter the same — a 18” tire won’t fit a 17” rim, period.
  2. Check rim width range — wider tires may not fit narrow rims safely.
  3. Verify clearance — wider tires need room in the swingarm and fairings.
  4. Load & speed ratings — matter for highway speeds and loaded touring.
  5. Match front/rear proportions — don’t put an extreme size change on both ends.

Tube-Type (TT) vs Tubeless (TL)

Every tire is marked as either TT (Tube Type) or TL (Tubeless):

  • TT (Tube-Type): Requires an inner tube. Common on dual-sport and classic bikes with spoked wheels.
  • TL (Tubeless): Seals directly to the rim. No tube needed. Standard on modern street bikes with cast alloy wheels.

📋 Before You Buy — Quick Checklist

  • Check your current tire's size (written on the sidewall)
  • Confirm TT or TL for your wheel type
  • Verify rim diameter matches exactly
  • Check load index for your bike weight
  • Consider riding conditions (street, off-road, mixed)

How to Choose the Right Tire

Selecting the right motorcycle tire depends on several factors:

  1. Riding style: Commuter, touring, off-road, sport
  2. Bike weight: Heavier bikes need higher load ratings
  3. Typical speed: Highway riders need higher speed ratings
  4. Road conditions: Wet, dry, mixed surfaces
  5. Budget: Premium vs. budget brands offer different longevity

When shopping for motorcycle tires online, always double-check the size format and make sure the tire matches your bike’s specifications. When in doubt, consult your owner’s manual or a professional tire dealer.
